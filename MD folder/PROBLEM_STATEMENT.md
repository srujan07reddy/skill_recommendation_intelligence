\## 1. Background



Universities consist of multiple academic departments with students possessing diverse technical, professional, domain-specific and practical competencies.



Students acquire these competencies through academic coursework, independent learning, projects, internships, certifications, workshops and other practical experiences.



However, universities often lack a unified mechanism to collect, consolidate and analyse this information at the departmental level.



Although individual students may possess valuable skills, the collective competency landscape of an academic department is not always clearly visible to university administrators and academic leadership.



Consequently, identifying common skill deficiencies, understanding proficiency distributions and planning targeted student development initiatives becomes difficult.



\## 2. Core Problem Statement



The absence of a centralized and structured student skill intelligence mechanism limits a university's ability to understand the collective competencies of students across its academic departments.



Without consolidated skill information and systematic analysis, Heads of Departments (HODs) and Deans face difficulties in identifying underdeveloped competencies, recognizing departmental skill gaps and determining appropriate student development initiatives.



Furthermore, decisions regarding technical workshops, certification programs, bootcamps, industry-oriented training and other development activities may depend on fragmented information, informal feedback and limited observations rather than comprehensive departmental skill analysis.



This creates a gap between the skills students currently possess and the competencies that academic departments aim to develop.



There is therefore a need for a platform that can systematically collect student skill information, analyse departmental competency patterns, identify development requirements and provide actionable recommendations to support institutional decision-making.



\## 3. Identified Problems



\### 3.1 Fragmented Student Skill Information



Student competencies are distributed across different sources, including academic activities, projects, internships, certifications and independent learning.



There is no unified analytical view of these competencies across departments.



\### 3.2 Limited Departmental Skill Visibility



HODs and Deans may not have a consolidated understanding of the skills possessed by students within their respective academic departments.



This makes it difficult to determine the overall competency distribution.



\### 3.3 Difficulty in Identifying Skill Gaps



Without structured proficiency analysis, identifying commonly underdeveloped skills and areas requiring additional training becomes challenging.



\### 3.4 Lack of Data-Driven Development Planning



Student development programs may be planned without sufficient visibility into actual departmental skill requirements.



This can result in training initiatives that do not adequately address the most relevant competency gaps.



\### 3.5 Difficulty in Prioritizing Development Initiatives



Academic leadership requires a way to compare skill deficiencies across departments and determine which development initiatives deserve priority.



Without consolidated analytical information, this decision-making process becomes less systematic.



\### 3.6 Lack of Consolidated Analytical Reporting



Universities need accessible reports that communicate departmental skill distributions, proficiency levels, identified deficiencies and recommended development activities.



Fragmented information makes institutional-level comparisons and reporting difficult.



\## 4. Proposed Problem Resolution



The Student Skill Intelligence Platform (SSIP) proposes a centralized, web-based analytical system that transforms student skill information into actionable departmental intelligence.



The platform will collect information through two input channels:



\*\*A. Student Skill Assessment Forms\*\*



Students provide structured information about their skills, proficiency levels, projects, practical experience, internships and certifications.



\*\*B. Bulk Dataset Upload\*\*



Authorized university officials can upload existing student skill datasets through Excel and CSV files.



The application processes both sources through a unified validation and normalization mechanism.



The collected information is then analysed to generate:



\- Department-wise skill distributions.

\- Proficiency-level distributions.

\- Common competency deficiencies.

\- Underdeveloped skill identification.

\- Departmental development requirements.

\- Recommended workshops and training programs.

\- Certification and bootcamp suggestions.



The resulting insights are presented through interactive dashboards accessible to authorized HODs and Deans.



HODs can review departmental findings and identify suitable development initiatives, while Deans can evaluate recommendations and make institutional-level decisions.



\## 5. Expected Problem Resolution Outcomes



The proposed platform aims to achieve the following outcomes:



1\. Improve visibility into the collective skill capabilities of students.

2\. Establish a structured approach to departmental competency analysis.

3\. Identify commonly underdeveloped skills.

4\. Support evidence-based student development planning.

5\. Improve prioritization of departmental training initiatives.

6\. Provide consolidated analytical reports to academic leadership.

7\. Connect identified skill deficiencies with relevant development programs.

8\. Enable university officials to preserve analytical results through downloadable reports and complete analysis packages.



\## 6. Intended Institutional Impact



SSIP aims to help universities transition from fragmented and observation-based student development planning towards structured, data-supported institutional decision-making.



By providing visibility into student competencies and departmental skill gaps, the platform can support more relevant training initiatives, better allocation of development resources and improved alignment between academic objectives and evolving industry requirements.



The platform is intended to function as a decision-support system for academic leadership rather than an autonomous authority for determining student competency or allocating institutional resources.
